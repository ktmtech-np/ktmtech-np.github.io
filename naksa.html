Naksa नक्सा

A self-hosted map of Nepal, built end to end from OpenStreetMap: our own vector tiles, search, and driving and walking directions, plus a map editor for the local detail OSM lacks. On top of it runs a working land-records system with the owners' portal, the land office (Malpot), the palika desk and the survey office (Napi), all in नेपाली and English.

Public map

Galli-style cartography drawn from our own tiles: land use, water, a road hierarchy, POI icons and 3D buildings, in light or dark, with labels in नेपाली or English. Nepal's border follows the official 2020 map. Place cards and "What's here?" give the full address: ward, municipality, district and province.

Routes go to a place's mapped entrance, with a dashed line for the last metres off the road. Closures and poor road conditions are respected, and the routing graph is rebuilt with no downtime.

Map editor

A workspace for adding what OSM lacks: houses, shops, galli alleys, entrances and road closures. OSM data itself is never changed. Edits are stored as overlays that survive re-imports, and they're re-attached by shape when OSM splits or renumbers a feature. Every change records who made it.

Land records: an "online Malpot"

On the same database runs a land-records system modelled on Nepal's real offices. Each office is its own app with its own address, logins and cookies, and all of them work one shared register. Staff sign in with a password plus a 6-digit code.

Demo data: every person, parcel and number is fictional, and every screen is marked "DEMO · not a legal record". Parcels are drawn around real buildings. Fees and deed formats are samples.

From application to new lalpurja: a parcel split

  1. Owner applies (Jagga)The owner draws a rough split line and types each piece's area. The server solves the exact lines to 0.01 m².
  2. Co-owners agreeEvery co-owner or heir acknowledges, online or at the office.
  3. Officer reviews (Malpot)Parties, documents and charges are checked, payment is taken, and a survey is requested.
  4. Survey measures (Napi)A surveyor enters the GNSS points of the line in the MUTM grid, and a checker approves the field book.
  5. Chief approvesThe chief sees exactly what will change in the register: four eyes on every change.
  6. Registered and sealedNew parcel numbers (kittas) and ownership certificates (lalpurja) are issued, the old ones are voided, and the change is sealed into a hash chain.

Jagga

जग्गाowners' portal

Owners see their parcels and the buildings on them, with joint and company land shown to every member. Areas appear in m², ropani-aana-paisa-daam and bigha-kattha-dhur. Owners start every change themselves: sale, gift, inheritance, partition, split, merge and more, then track it step by step.

Malpot

मालपोतLand Administration Office

Staff work from a queue that shows what each application is waiting for. An officer checks identity, documents and land tax, calculates fees and capital gains tax, takes payments and issues receipts. The chief then approves. Every deed becomes a likhat, and approved changes are sealed into a tamper-evident hash chain. The office also handles mortgages, court orders, tenants, certified copies and a printable day book.

Napi

नापीSurvey Office

A split is measured on the ground before it's registered. The survey office holds map sheets, control points and append-only field books, and it handles boundary marking, disputes and land-acquisition surveys. It also has a public kitta look-up that needs no login.

Palika

पालिकाlocal land desk

The same land-office services offered by local government, with its own staff, working on the same register. Here a tenancy is ended and sent to the palika chief for approval.

नेपाली and English

Every app can be switched between the two languages on every page. In Nepali mode it uses Devanagari digits, Bikram Sambat dates and ropani or bigha, and office terms follow the real Malpot and Napi forms.

Architecture

Everything is stored in PostGIS and transformed to Web Mercator only for tiles. The land apps reach map data only through SQL API functions, and each program has its own database login.

Backend

  • Go 1.26: one module, vendored dependencies
  • PostgreSQL 16 + PostGIS 3.4
  • goose migrations plus repeatable SQL views

Map pipeline

  • osm2pgsql with a Lua flex style
  • Vector tiles via ST_AsMVT, gzipped with ETags
  • OSRM rebuilt next to the live graph, then swapped in

Front end and ops

  • MapLibre GL JS, plain JavaScript modules, no build step
  • Caddy, Docker Compose, k3s
  • Playwright tests on a separate test stack

Engineering decisions

OSM stays read-only; our changes are overlays

Each edit remembers its feature's shape and is re-attached after every OSM import. Unclear cases go to a review list, because guessing silently would be worse than asking.

Getting our alleys into routing

OSRM only reads OSM files, and roads connect only through shared nodes. So the routing input is regenerated with junction nodes inserted wherever our alleys meet OSM roads.

A 50 m closure blocks 50 m

Routing rules split roads at their ends instead of closing whole OSM ways, which are often hundreds of metres long. Rules are matched by shape at every rebuild, so they survive OSM updates.

Four-eyes approval and a hash chain

A land change is tried out in a transaction that's rolled back, then approved by a second person, then sealed into a hash chain. Direct database edits become detectable without a blockchain.

Node.js → Go with identical hashes

The land ledger's hashes were computed from JavaScript's JSON output. The Go port reproduces JSON.stringify exactly, so every old record still verifies.

Datum shifts are explicit

In Nepal, PROJ's default Everest → WGS 84 conversion silently applies no shift, which is about 148 m of error. Uploads therefore accept a custom PROJ string for MUTM and Everest data.

Search and tiles at national scale

Trigram indexes on the live OSM tables keep search at 70–210 ms over all of Nepal without a copy that goes stale. Zoomed-out tiles are simplified and precomputed, which cut zoom 8 from 900 ms to 164 ms.
